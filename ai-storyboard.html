Section 1 · Good fit or not
2.0

What AI is good at, and what it's not

Content

On-screen text

Good fit: drafting emails and documents, summarizing long text, changing tone, brainstorming ideas, explaining something in simpler words.

Be careful: exact facts and figures, math, recent events and prices, decisions about people, and anything that depends on company info you haven't given it.

Narration

"AI assistants are great with words: drafting, summarizing, rewording, brainstorming. They're much weaker with facts, numbers and judgment calls. They can sound sure of themselves and still be wrong."

2.1

Sort Maya's tasks

Sorting activity

On-screen text

Maya has six tasks. For each one, choose Good fit or Be careful.

  • Draft a friendly reminder email about a deadline Good fit
  • Turn her own meeting notes into 5 bullet points Good fit
  • Brainstorm names for a team event Good fit
  • Look up a vendor's current price Be careful
  • Add up quarterly budget totals for a report Be careful
  • Decide which employee should get a promotion Be careful

Feedback (per item, after each choice)

  • Reminder email: drafting is a sweet spot. Maya just reviews the tone.
  • Meeting notes: summarizing her own notes works well, since AI is working from real info she provided.
  • Event names: brainstorming is low-risk and fast.
  • Vendor price: AI may give an outdated or made-up number. Check the vendor's site.
  • Budget totals: AI can make math mistakes. Use a spreadsheet.
  • Promotion: decisions about people need human judgment and context AI doesn't have.

Notes

Two big buttons per task (no drag and drop), so it works with keyboard, touch and screen readers. Shows the score at the end, e.g. "You got 5 of 6."

Section 2 · Better prompts
3.0

Why vague prompts get vague answers

Content

On-screen text

Weak prompt: "Write an email about the order."

Result: a generic email that doesn't say what's happening, who it's for or what to do next.

A strong prompt has three parts:

  • Goal: what do you want?
  • Context: what does the AI need to know?
  • Format: what should the answer look like?

The fourth part, Check, comes after the AI answers. More on that in Section 4.

Narration

"The AI only knows what you tell it. A one-line prompt gets a one-size-fits-all answer. Give it a clear goal, the context it needs, and the format you want, and the first draft gets a lot closer."

3.1

Prompt makeover

Build activity

On-screen text

A customer's order is delayed and Maya needs to email them. Build her prompt by picking the best option for each part.

Goal

  • Write something about the order
  • ✓ Write a short apology email telling a customer their order is delayed by one week
  • Help me with customers

Context

  • Customers are important to us
  • ✓ The customer is a long-time client, the delay is on our supplier's end, and we're offering free shipping on their next order
  • No context needed, the AI knows our business

Format

  • Make it good
  • ✓ Under 120 words, warm but professional, end with [My name] and [Phone]
  • As long as possible

Feedback

Per wrong pick, e.g.: "Too vague. The AI won't know it's a delay or that it's an apology."

When all three are right, show the assembled prompt and a sample AI response: a short, warm apology email that explains the one-week delay, offers free shipping and ends with the placeholders.

"See the difference? Same tool, much better first draft."

Notes

Learners can retry each part until it's right. The assembled prompt builds up live as they choose.

Section 3 · Protect data
4.0

What never goes into an AI tool

Content

On-screen text

Keep out:

  • Customer personal info (names with account numbers, addresses, ID numbers)
  • Passwords and login details
  • Confidential financials and unreleased plans
  • HR or medical info about coworkers

Quick test: Would you be OK if this showed up outside the company?

Better habit: swap real details for placeholders like [Customer] or [Account #].

Always follow your company's AI policy. Approved internal tools may have different rules.

Narration

"Whatever you paste into an AI tool may be stored, and depending on the tool, it may be used for training. So before you paste, ask yourself one question: would I be OK if this showed up outside the company? If not, take the sensitive details out, or don't paste it."

4.1

Share or don't share

Decision activity

On-screen text

Maya wants to paste each of these into a public AI tool. Is it OK?

  • A press release that's already public, to shorten it OK to share
  • A customer's name, address and account number, to write them a letter Don't share
  • Her login password, to get help with an error Don't share
  • A general question: how to write a polite follow-up email OK to share
  • Draft quarterly results before they're announced Don't share

Feedback

  • Press release: already public, so there's no risk.
  • Customer details: use placeholders instead, and fill in the real details yourself afterward.
  • Password: never share it with any tool or person. Describe the error message instead.
  • General question: no company info involved.
  • Unreleased results: confidential until announced. Only use tools your company has approved for this.
Section 4 · Check the output
5.0

AI can be confidently wrong

Content

On-screen text

AI sometimes makes things up: names, numbers, dates, policies, even links. It sounds right, which makes it easy to miss. This is called a "hallucination."

The 3-step check:

  • Facts: verify names, numbers, dates and policies against a real source.
  • Fit: does it say what you mean, in the right tone?
  • Final read: read it as the person receiving it.

Narration

"AI doesn't look things up the way you'd expect. It predicts what sounds right. Most of the time that works, but sometimes it invents details. You're still the one sending it, so check the facts, check the fit, and give it a final read."

5.1

Spot the errors

Hotspot activity

On-screen text

Maya's notes: new expense policy starts Monday. Submit by the 15th of each month. Receipts needed for anything over $25. Manager approval still required. Questions go to Finance.

AI draft: click each sentence that doesn't match her notes. There are 3.

  • "Our new expense policy starts this Monday." ✓
  • "Please submit expenses by the 30th of each month." Error
  • "Receipts are required for purchases over $50." Error
  • "Good news: manager approval is no longer required." Error
  • "Send any questions to the Finance team." ✓

Feedback

  • 30th: her notes say the 15th. A wrong deadline means late expenses.
  • $50: the notes say $25. People would skip receipts they need.
  • Approval: the AI invented this. It would cause real problems.
  • Correct sentence clicked: "That one matches her notes. Keep looking."

When all 3 are found: "If Maya had sent this as-is, her whole team would have the wrong rules. Always check the facts."

Wrap-up
6.0

Knowledge check

Quiz · 4 questions

Questions (correct answer marked)

  • Q1. Which task is the best fit for an AI assistant? Drafting a first version of a team announcement / Calculating payroll / Choosing who to lay off / Confirming today's stock price
  • Q2. Which prompt has a goal, context and format? "Write a 3-sentence thank-you note to a client who referred us a new customer. Keep it warm and casual." / "Write a thank-you note." / "Be nice to clients." / "Thank-you note, please."
  • Q3. A coworker pastes a full customer list into a public AI tool to "clean it up." Best response? Stop, remove the personal info and check the company AI policy / It's fine if it saves time / Only worry if the list is huge / Delete the chat and move on
  • Q4. The AI gives you a statistic for your report. What do you do? Find and confirm it in a reliable source before using it / Use it, AI is usually right / Round it to make it look cleaner / Ask the AI if it's sure

Feedback

Immediate feedback after each answer that explains why. Q4 also addresses the "ask the AI if it's sure" choice: "The AI may just repeat itself confidently. Check a real source."

Scoring

Passing: 3 of 4 (75%). Below that, learners can retake the check.

6.1

Recap and job aid

Summary

On-screen text

Score shown. Recap of the four habits:

  • Goal: say what you want
  • Context: give it what it needs
  • Format: describe the answer you want
  • Check: verify before you use it

Plus: keep sensitive data out.

Buttons: Print the job aid · Restart

Narration

"That's it. Goal, Context, Format, Check, and keep sensitive data out. Print the one-page job aid and keep it near your desk. Maya does."

Notes

Job aid is a one-page printable card with the framework, the "keep out" list and the 3-step check. Completion would be reported to the LMS (SCORM/xAPI) in a production version.
