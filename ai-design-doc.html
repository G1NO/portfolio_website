Using AI Assistants at Work: A Practical Starter Course

A short, self-paced e-learning module that teaches non-technical employees how to get useful results from AI assistants without putting company data or their own credibility at risk.

AudienceNon-technical employees
FormatSelf-paced e-learning
Seat timeAbout 15 minutes
ModelADDIE, rapid prototype

01The problem

Most companies have handed employees AI tools like ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot or Claude, or employees are already using them on their own. Either way, people are learning by trial and error. That leads to three predictable problems:

  • Weak results. People type one vague sentence, get a generic answer, and decide the tool "doesn't work."
  • Data risk. People paste customer details, financials or internal documents into tools without knowing where that data goes.
  • Unchecked errors. AI can state wrong information confidently. Employees who don't know that pass mistakes along to customers and leadership.

Long technical courses don't fix this. These employees don't need to know how a language model works. They need a few habits they can use the same day.

Performance goal: After the course, employees use AI assistants for everyday tasks, write prompts that get usable results on the first or second try, keep sensitive information out of the tools, and check AI output before they rely on it.

03Learning objectives

By the end of the module, learners will be able to:

  1. Identify which everyday tasks AI assistants handle well and which they handle poorly.
  2. Write a clear prompt using a four-part framework (Goal, Context, Format, Check).
  3. Decide what information is safe to share with an AI tool and what is not.
  4. Review AI output for errors before using it.

The core framework

The whole course is built around one memorable model that fits on a sticky note:

GGoal: what do you want?
CContext: what does it need to know?
FFormat: what should the answer look like?
CCheck: review it before you use it

04Instructional approach

Strategy

Scenario-based and hands-on. Learners follow a relatable character, a busy coordinator named Maya, through one workday where she uses AI for real tasks. Every concept is introduced through her situation and then practiced right away. Learners do something on almost every screen instead of reading slides.

Structure

SectionContentPractice activity
IntroMeet Maya and her packed morning. Why AI skills matter now.Quick poll: how do you use AI today?
1. Good fit or notWhat AI is good at (drafting, summarizing, brainstorming) and where it struggles (facts, math, current events, judgment calls)Sort 6 tasks into "Good fit" or "Be careful"
2. Better promptsThe Goal, Context, Format framework, with before-and-after examplesPrompt makeover: build a stronger prompt from parts
3. Protect dataWhat never goes into an AI tool, and the "would I post it on the break room wall?" testShare or don't share: 5 quick decisions
4. Check the outputWhy AI makes things up, and a 3-step checkSpot the errors in an AI-written email
Wrap-upFinal scenario and recap4-question knowledge check, plus a downloadable job aid

Design principles

  • Show, then do. Every concept gets an example first, then an activity.
  • Immediate feedback that explains why, not just right or wrong.
  • Plain language at about an 8th-grade reading level.
  • Accessible: keyboard navigable, readable contrast, no information carried by color alone, works with screen readers.
  • Performance support: a one-page Goal, Context, Format, Check job aid learners keep at their desk.

05Evaluation plan

Built around the Kirkpatrick model so the course can show impact, not just completions.

LevelQuestionMeasure
1. ReactionDid learners find it useful and relevant?3-question survey at the end of the module
2. LearningCan they apply the framework?Knowledge check scores (target: 80% or higher)
3. BehaviorAre they using AI better on the job?30-day follow-up survey, plus manager check-ins on AI use and data incidents
4. ResultsDid it help the business?Time saved on routine writing tasks, and fewer data-handling incidents involving AI tools

06Development plan

  1. Analysis: audience profile, task list and objectives (this document)
  2. Design: screen-by-screen storyboard with narration, on-screen text and interactions
  3. Development: responsive HTML module with built-in interactions and feedback
  4. Review: test with 3–5 people from the target audience, then revise
  5. Launch and evaluate: publish, then collect Level 1–3 data

Tools: storyboard written in a doc; module built as a responsive web page (could also be built in Articulate Rise or Storyline from the same storyboard).
